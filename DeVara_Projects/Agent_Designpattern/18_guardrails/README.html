Chapter 18 — Guardrails / Safety Patterns

Filter the input, constrain the prompt, scrub the output. Refuse, redact, or escalate when needed.

DecoratorChain of ResponsibilitySafety

What is it?

Guardrails sit on three sides of the LLM:

  • Input — sanitization, prompt-injection defense, PII redaction.
  • Behavioral — system prompt, tool allow-list, max steps, scope rules.
  • Output — toxicity, hallucination, schema validation, secret leakage.
Mental model: bouncers at the door + rules inside + final pat-down on the way out.

2026 real-time scenario — Healthcare triage chatbot

A 2026 hospital chatbot must never diagnose, prescribe, or expose other patients' data. Input filters strip PII before sending to the LLM, the system prompt forbids medical advice, output filters catch any attempt to give a dosage and replace with "please consult your doctor."

Interview Q&A

How do you defend against prompt injection?
Treat retrieved content as untrusted; enforce structured outputs; isolate tool privileges; use Llama Guard or a dedicated classifier; never give the LLM raw access to credentials.
Is moderation enough?
No — moderation catches unsafe text but not policy violations specific to your domain (e.g., "no investment advice"). Layer general moderation + domain-specific rails.
