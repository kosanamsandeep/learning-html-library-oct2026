Security, Overhang & the Human Moat

Three independent ideas that round out the picture: how injection resistance actually works, how to diagnose your harness, and what part of your job can't be automated.

Three-Layer Prompt-Injection Defense

Cherny claims Claude Code is effectively no longer "prompt injectable" — but he attributes that to a defense architecture, not just a better model. You can replicate the pattern:

graph TB INJ["Prompt injection attempt"] --> L1["Layer 1: well-aligned model
refuses / doesn't follow injected instructions"] L1 -->|"slips past"| L2["Layer 2: dedicated classifier
scans all traffic"] L2 -->|"slips past"| L3["Layer 3: mechanistic interpretability
monitors specific 'neurons' for injection"] L1 -->|"blocked"| STOP["✅ Blocked"] L2 -->|"blocked"| STOP L3 -->|"blocked"| STOP

The three layers

LayerWhat it doesCan you build it?
1. Well-aligned base modelThe model itself refuses injection — the first and biggest filter⚠️ You choose the model; you can't tune its alignment directly
2. Injection classifierA dedicated classifier running across all traffic detects injection patterns✅ Yes — a secondary model/labeled classifier over inputs & tool outputs
3. Mechanistic interpretabilityMonitors specific internal "neurons" associated with injection attempts❌ Frontier-lab research — you can't replicate this, but you can design layers 1–2 and assume layer 3 on frontier models
Note: treat "we're no longer prompt injectable" as a strong claim, not a guarantee. Defense-in-depth means no single layer is trusted. For your own agents, the practical takeaway is the classifier layer — and never trusting raw tool output (already in your security model).

Overhang vs Underhang — the Diagnostic

Product Overhang

Model can do more than your product lets it express.

  • Symptom: results are worse than the raw model in a playground
  • Cause: harness constraints, over-specified scaffolding
  • Fix: unhobble — simpler, higher-level access (full terminal, broader tools)

Product Underhang

You're forcing a model beyond its current capability.

  • Symptom: results are poor no matter the prompt
  • Cause: task exceeds model ceiling
  • Fix: more scaffolding, domain-specific harness, or a stronger model
Diagnose before you change anything: most teams assume underhang ("the model can't do this") and add scaffolding, when they actually have overhang ("the model can, my harness is blocking it") — and the added scaffolding makes it worse. Run the raw model in a playground on the same task to find out which side you're on.

The Human Moat: What Won't Be Automated

When an agentic workflow can rewrite an entire 100K-line codebase in 11 days, the engineering role shifts dramatically. The non-automatable core:

  1. Deciding what should exist in the first place — the product/architecture judgment of what's worth building.
  2. Specifying a system well enough that an agent can build it — the task + guardrails + exit criteria skill (Module 02).
  3. Functional systems requirements, design sense, and architecture — the shape of the system, not its implementation.
Implication for you: as a senior AI engineer, double down on design sense and requirements-elicitation. The engineer who can precisely specify what to build — and define verification for it — controls the agents that do the how. That's the moat.

The Complete Elicitation-Era Playbook

  1. Ablate first — delete prompts/skills/hooks, test raw, re-add only on failure. (Module 01)
  2. Define, don't prescribe — task + guardrails + exit criteria, never a step-by-step manual. (Module 02)
  3. Build verification in — test suites, pixel comparisons, compile gates the agent feeds back on. (Module 02)
  4. Scale compute with stakes — sequence / parallelise / fan-out-and-verify, and spend test-time compute only where it pays. (Module 03)
  5. Automate maintenance — loops locally, routines in the cloud, each with a verification loop. (Module 04)
  6. Defend in depth — alignment + classifier + (on frontier) interpretability; never trust raw tool output. (Module 05)
  7. Own the specification — the human moat is defining what should exist and how it verifies success. (Module 05)
