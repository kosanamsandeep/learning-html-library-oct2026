03 · Attention Scores — Q · KT & Scaling

Now that we have Q, K, V, the model computes pairwise affinity scores — how much each token should attend to every other token.

🔗 Step 2: Dot-Product Affinity

Step 2

Q · KT — Every Query × Every Key

We take the dot product between each token's Query and every token's Key. A high dot product means "this Query found a highly relevant Key."

Raw Scores = Q · KT

Q: (3×2), K: (3×2) → KT: (2×3) → Result: (3×3)

      Kthe   Kcat   Ksat
Qthe   ⎡0.07   0.55   0.39⎤
Qcat   ⎢0.32   1.92   1.04⎥
Qsat   ⎣0.15   1.11   0.75⎦

Reading the matrix:

Qcat × Kcat = 1.92
→ "cat" finds itself highly relevant (top-left of its row) — always attends strongly to itself
Qcat × Ksat = 1.04
→ "cat" also finds "sat" relevant (they're neighbors in the sentence)
Qcat × Kthe = 0.32
→ Lower relevance — "the" is a function word with less semantic content

📏 Step 2b: Scaling by √dk

Why?

The Softmax Saturation Problem

Dot products grow large with higher dimensions. When dk is large, the variance of the dot product increases, pushing softmax into regions with extremely sharp gradients — making it hard to learn.

Variance(Q·KT) = dk  →  StdDev = √dk Scaled = (Q · KT) / √dk
Dimension dkUnscaled Variance√dkEffect If Unscaled
2 (our example)21.414Mild — softmax works fine
64 (typical)648Dot products in range ~[-30, 30] — softmax almost 1-hot
128 (large model)12811.3Severe saturation — gradients effectively zero

Scaling by √dk normalizes the variance back to ~1, keeping softmax in the "sigmoid" region where gradients flow well.

Our Example

√dk = √2 ≈ 1.414 Scaled Scores = Raw / 1.414 ⎡0.05   0.39   0.28⎤ ⎢0.22   1.35   0.73⎥ ⎣0.10   0.78   0.53⎦

🔑 Key insight: The score matrix is not symmetric. Qcat·Ksat (0.73) can differ from Qsat·Kcat (0.78). This is by design — "what cat wants from sat" is different from "what sat wants from cat." The two tokens have different informational needs at their positions.

🔍 What Makes a Good Score?

During training, the WQ and WK weights are tuned so that:

  • High scores for tokens that should share information (subject-verb, noun-adjective)
  • Low scores for unrelated token pairs
  • Multiple heads learn different notions of relevance (syntax vs semantics vs proximity)
