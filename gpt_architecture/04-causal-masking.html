04 · Causal Masking & Softmax

Two critical operations transform raw scores into a usable attention distribution: causal masking prevents cheating, and softmax turns scores into probabilities.

🚫 Step 3: Causal Masking

Step 3

Why Mask?

In a decoder-only model (GPT, Claude, Llama), tokens must predict the next token using only previous tokens. A token at position i must not see tokens at positions >i — otherwise it could cheat by looking at the answer.

⚠️ What happens without masking? If "sat" can attend to "on" (the next token), the model learns nothing — it just copies the next token's representation. The loss goes to zero trivially, but the model has zero generative ability.

We enforce this by setting the upper triangle of the score matrix to -∞ (negative infinity):

📌 The Mask Pattern: Position i can attend to positions j ≤ i only. Every token sees itself and all previous tokens. Nothing beyond its position.

📊 Step 4: Softmax — Scores → Probabilities

Step 4

Softmax Makes Each Row Sum to 1

Softmax takes a vector of scores (including -∞ values) and converts them into probabilities that sum to 1.0. After this, each row tells us: "given this token's query, what percentage of attention goes to each earlier token?"

softmax(xi) = exp(xi) / Σj exp(xj) Where -∞ becomes exp(-∞) = 0 — masked positions get exactly 0% attention.

🧐 Interpreting the Attention Distribution

Analysis

Row by Row

  • "the" (row 1): [1.00, 0.00, 0.00] — The first token can only attend to itself. All attention is self-attention.
  • "cat" (row 2): [0.24, 0.76, 0.00] — "cat" looks mostly at itself (76%), but also gives 24% to "the". The model has learned that determiners carry useful information about their nouns.
  • "sat" (row 3): [0.18, 0.51, 0.31] — "sat" gives a whopping 51% to "cat" (the subject) and 18% to "the" (the determiner). Only 31% to itself. This is attention working perfectly: the verb looks to its subject for agreement.

🔑 Key takeaway: Softmax amplifies differences. A score of 1.35 vs 0.73 (ratio ~1.85×) becomes probabilities 0.76 vs 0.24 (ratio ~3.2×). This competitive normalization is what makes attention selective — tokens must compete for attention, and the most relevant ones win.
