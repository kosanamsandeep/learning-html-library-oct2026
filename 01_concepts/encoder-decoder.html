Step 3

Encoder decoder architecture

Main idea: the encoder understands the source sequence, and the decoder generates the target sequence while consulting the encoder output through cross-attention. This is the classic sequence-to-sequence transformer design used by the original Transformer paper and models such as T5.

Why keep an encoder?

Some tasks need a deep understanding of a complete input before generation begins. Translation is the clearest example: the source sentence should be processed as a whole.

Why keep a decoder?

The output must still be generated one token at a time. The decoder uses causal masking so it only sees previously generated output tokens.

In and out

Flow of information

1. Source sequence enters encoder

Example source: "The meeting starts at noon." The encoder turns this into source-side contextual vectors.

2. Target prefix enters decoder

During training, the decoder sees earlier target tokens. During inference, it sees the tokens it has already generated.

3. Decoder cross-attends to encoder states

The decoder asks: which parts of the source are relevant for predicting the next target token right now?

4. Output token is predicted

The decoder outputs logits for the next token, which is sampled or greedily selected.

Cross-attention is the bridge

In self-attention, queries, keys, and values all come from the same sequence. In cross-attention, decoder states provide the queries while encoder states provide the keys and values.

Q = decoder states, K = encoder states, V = encoder states

Concrete example

English to French translation

Source: "The red car is fast."

The encoder builds a structured representation of the full English sentence. The decoder begins with a start token, predicts "La", then "voiture", then "rouge", and so on. At each step, cross-attention lets the decoder focus on the relevant source words instead of relying only on its own partial output history.

Architecture Best at Main tradeoff
Encoder-only Understanding and representation Not naturally generative.
Encoder-decoder Structured input-to-output generation More complex and heavier than decoder-only generation.
Decoder-only GPT General autoregressive text generation No dedicated encoder view of the source.

Architecture choice

Why do sequence-to-sequence tasks often need encoder-decoder models?

Why this design is strong

  • The encoder can build a global understanding of the source before any target token is emitted.
  • The decoder can remain autoregressive while still consulting the full source through cross-attention.
  • This cleanly separates source comprehension from target generation.

What alternatives fail at

  • A plain encoder cannot generate a rich target sequence by itself.
  • A plain decoder-only model can be prompted with source text, but it has no dedicated source-side latent representation and can be less structured for formal transduction tasks.
  • Older seq2seq RNNs struggle more with long dependencies and parallelization.

Interview readiness

Questionnaire and scenarios

Question

What exactly does cross-attention buy you over self-attention alone?

Strong answer

It lets the decoder query a separately encoded source representation. Self-attention only mixes information inside one sequence, but cross-attention lets the target-generation process align itself to relevant source content at each step.

Question

Why is encoder-decoder still useful when GPT models can also do translation?

Strong answer

Because tasks with a well-defined source-to-target mapping often benefit from an explicit source representation and source-target alignment. GPT can imitate this through prompting, but encoder-decoder models structure the problem more directly.

Scenario

You must summarize long legal documents into short plain-language answers. Why might encoder-decoder be preferable?

Expected reasoning

The full document can be encoded as source context, while the decoder generates a concise answer that repeatedly attends back to the source. This is a better fit than relying only on a decoder prompt when faithful grounding is critical.

Scenario

If translation quality is poor, what component-specific diagnosis would you mention in an interview?

Expected reasoning

Check source encoding quality, cross-attention alignment, causal decoding behavior, tokenization mismatch, and whether the model is losing source coverage. Poor translation is often not just “bad language modeling”; it can be bad source-to-target alignment.
