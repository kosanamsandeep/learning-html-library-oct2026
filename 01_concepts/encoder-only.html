Step 2

Encoder to encoder architecture

Meaning here: a stack made only of encoder blocks, where the sequence enters, gets contextualized layer by layer, and exits as contextual embeddings. This is the pattern used by BERT-like models.

What an encoder-only transformer does

Each layer uses bidirectional self-attention. A token can attend to tokens on both its left and right, so the final representation is deeply contextual.

What it produces

The output is not usually a generated sentence. It is a sequence of learned vectors that can be sent into a task-specific head.

In and out

How information flows

Input

Token embeddings plus positional embeddings enter the first encoder block.

Context building

Every layer updates every token using information from the entire sentence. Later layers usually represent more abstract meaning than earlier layers.

Output

The final hidden states can be pooled for sentence-level tasks or kept token-by-token for structured prediction tasks.

tokens -> embeddings -> encoder stack -> contextual vectors -> task head

Concrete example

Sentiment classification with an encoder

Input: "The headphones look premium but sound flat."

The token "flat" attends to "sound". The token "premium" attends to "look". The model learns that visual praise and audio criticism are separate ideas. A pooled representation can then feed a classifier that predicts mixed or negative sentiment depending on the label scheme.

Why encoder-only models are strong Typical tasks
Full bidirectional context gives strong understanding of a sentence. Classification, retrieval, ranking, embedding generation.
Outputs are stable vector representations rather than generated text. Semantic search, clustering, sentence similarity.
Can label each token with rich context. Named entity recognition, part-of-speech tagging.

Key limitation

Encoder-only stacks are optimized for understanding, not open-ended generation. They produce rich representations, but they do not naturally autoregress one token at a time like GPT models.

Architecture choice

Why choose encoder-only instead of decoder-only or classical methods?

Why encoder-only is the right choice for understanding tasks

  • Bidirectional attention lets each token use both left and right context.
  • That full context is ideal when the job is to classify, rank, retrieve, or tag rather than generate.
  • The output vectors are stable representations that can be reused across many downstream tasks.

What other approaches often fail at

  • Decoder-only models can do understanding, but they do not naturally expose a symmetric bidirectional encoding objective.
  • RNN encoders capture context more slowly and less efficiently at large scale.
  • Classical feature engineering or TF-IDF misses rich token interaction and polysemy.

Interview readiness

Questionnaire and scenarios

Question

Why is bidirectional context such a big advantage for BERT-style models?

Strong answer

Because many understanding tasks depend on both earlier and later words. For token disambiguation, sentiment, and entity labeling, seeing both sides of the token produces a cleaner contextual representation than a left-to-right view alone.

Question

Why not just use a decoder-only model for sentence embeddings?

Strong answer

You can, but encoder-only models are usually more natural for this because the entire sequence is encoded symmetrically. Decoder-only hidden states are optimized for next-token prediction, not primarily for a balanced sentence-level representation.

Scenario

You need semantic search over product descriptions, with fast offline embedding generation. Which architecture fits best?

Expected reasoning

Encoder-only. The target is representation quality, not generation. You want a compact contextual vector for each description that can be indexed and compared efficiently.

Scenario

An interviewer asks why encoder-only models are weak at open-ended text generation. What do you say?

Expected reasoning

Because they are not organized around autoregressive decoding. Their hidden states are excellent for understanding the full input sequence, but they do not naturally implement the stepwise next-token generation loop that decoder-only systems optimize for.
