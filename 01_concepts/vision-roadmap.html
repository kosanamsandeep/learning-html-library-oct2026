Learning path

Roadmap for computer vision, multimodal LLMs, and generative video

This roadmap is designed for someone who wants to understand how today\'s vision systems work, how multimodal assistants reason over images and video, and how to move from fundamentals to production-grade projects.

The correct strategy is not to start with image-to-video generation directly. Start with representations, tasks, and system design first. Otherwise the field feels magical instead of understandable.

Roadmap principle

Learn in this order: image representation -> supervised CV tasks -> transformers -> foundation models -> multimodal reasoning -> video -> production

What competence should look like

You should be able to explain why a CNN works, when to use ViT or CLIP, how a multimodal LLM sees an image, when segmentation needs a specialist model, how video summarization is built, and what makes inference fast enough for product use.

What not to do

Do not memorize model names without understanding the task families: classification, detection, segmentation, retrieval, captioning, visual grounding, and temporal reasoning. The task framing matters more than a benchmark leaderboard snapshot.

Stage 1

Foundations

Learn image representation basics

Pixels, channels, spatial locality, convolution intuition, receptive field, feature maps, pooling, augmentation, normalization, and common losses such as cross-entropy.

Learn the core math and training pieces

Linear algebra, convolution as a local weighted operation, backpropagation, SGD/Adam, and why regularization and augmentation matter.

Understand evaluation

Classification accuracy, precision, recall, F1, confusion matrix, IoU, mAP, and why metrics differ between classification, detection, and segmentation.

Stage 2

Classical CV to CNNs

Study order

  • Handcrafted features: edges, HOG, SIFT, ORB.
  • LeNet and the CNN idea.
  • AlexNet, VGG, Inception, ResNet.
  • Transfer learning and fine-tuning.

What to build

Build an image classifier on a small dataset, then fine-tune a pretrained CNN on a domain dataset such as retail products, waste classification, food images, or plant disease.

Stage 3

Detection and segmentation

Task What to learn Typical models
Object detection Boxes, anchors, IoU, NMS, one-stage vs two-stage detection. Faster R-CNN, YOLO family, DETR.
Semantic segmentation Per-pixel classification and mask losses. U-Net, DeepLab.
Instance / promptable segmentation Separate object masks and promptable mask generation. Mask R-CNN, SAM.

Stage 4

Vision transformers and foundation models

Learn patch embeddings and self-attention

Understand how ViT converts images into patch tokens and why attention helps with global context.

Study CLIP, DINO, MAE, and SAM

These models show how vision moved from single-task supervision to reusable foundation representations.

Learn transfer, prompting, and zero-shot behavior

Modern systems are valuable because they can generalize far beyond a single labeled dataset.

Stage 5

Multimodal LLMs and video

Multimodal path

  • Vision encoder + projector + LLM architecture.
  • Visual question answering and captioning.
  • OCR-plus-reasoning and document understanding.
  • Tool-augmented vision systems for segmentation and retrieval.

Video path

  • Frame sampling and clip encoding.
  • Temporal transformers and memory.
  • Action recognition and event summarization.
  • Image-to-video and text-to-video generation basics.

Stage 6

Production systems

Serving and latency

Model selection, batching, quantization, ONNX/TensorRT, GPU memory planning, and streaming responses.

Evaluation and monitoring

Benchmark by task, monitor drift, inspect failure cases, and create human review loops for high-risk cases.

Pipeline composition

Use specialist models where needed: detector + OCR + retrieval + LLM summarizer is often better than one huge model trying to do everything.

Project roadmap

Interview advice

How to explain this area in interviews

What strong answers sound like

  • Explain the representation learning story, not only model names.
  • Distinguish classification, detection, segmentation, retrieval, captioning, and video reasoning.
  • Know why multimodal LLMs need vision encoders and often specialist tools.
  • Know why production systems care about latency, batching, and quantization.

What weak answers sound like

  • Calling every image task “just a vision transformer.”
  • Saying video is just images repeated without temporal reasoning issues.
  • Assuming one multimodal LLM solves segmentation, OCR, and tracking equally well.
  • Ignoring evaluation and deployment constraints.

Final roadmap summary

Learn the tasks first, then CNNs, then transformers and foundation models, then multimodal and video reasoning, and finally production deployment. The field becomes manageable when you see it as a progression of reusable representations, not a list of disconnected model names.

This roadmap takes you from vision basics to multimodal and generative video systems in a usable order.
